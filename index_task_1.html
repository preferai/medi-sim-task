Classify a large volume of new medical content automatically.

Step 2 · What the errors look like

Errors are mostly wrong values and not invented ones.

Glued names

KamilLisekCarolineDive

Altered letters

SfllomosHUGSλ3-integrin

The page says Sflomos, HUG and β3-integrin.

Category guessed

Taken from how the title sounds instead of the role label printed next to it.

Mechanical errors, fixable in code first: split names, require every value to appear in the source.

The judge needs checking too: a second run of the same judge built a different reference (41 talks instead of 38, precision 0.974). Next: an ensemble of smaller judges with majority or average voting (JudgeBlender, Rahmani et al. 2025) plus a human spot check.

Step 3 · Topics from the abstracts

Topics need text to read.

A script follows the extracted URLs, crawls the abstracts and asks the same small model (thinking off) for a topics list. A bigger model would do better; the small one saves tokens.

Step 4 · Explore before you trust

Clusters find talk formats before subjects.

One cluster is closing remarks, awards and opening addresses. Silhouette is 0.061 at k = 3 (0.056 to 0.071 for k = 2 to 7), so structure is weak, as expected with 38 talks.

Next: medical experts judge which topics and groups make sense and fix the vocabulary. Their decisions become the ground truth.

Step 5 · Cost now, cheaper later

Pay for accuracy until the vocabulary settles.

Measure first

Cost and turnaround were not measured in the PoC. Log tokens and time per record with LiteLLM or Codeburn.

Larger models for now

Until the folksonomy is stable, tracked by the velocity of novel topics. Today every topic string is unique.

Cheap classifiers later

Train on the learned topics: XGBoost as a baseline, JEV worth testing.

Dominance as a signal

The UK has 8 of 38 talks. If that holds across conferences and correlates with engagement, it can boost or damp content.

Step 6 · From folksonomy to taxonomy

New content gets tags from similar old content.

Extracted topics are checked against a fixed taxonomy. Two ways to assign tags at scale:

Trained classifier

XGBoost on the learned topics, once the folksonomy is stable (Step 5).

kNN tag recommendation

Find similar, already reviewed content in Elasticsearch and reuse its topics.

To test: bag of words, dense embeddings, sparse embeddings.

The human loop stays: low-confidence records, failing URLs and new topics go to experts, and their decisions feed the ground truth from Steps 1 and 4.
